# 06 — Operating Model (LinkedIn Pipeline)

## Charter

The LinkedIn unit runs the content pipeline for a 6-week build-in-
public sprint (optionally tied to a course or cohort you're taking —
name it in the worksheet), positioning the owner as a top-tier
[YOUR TARGET ROLE] candidate. Pipeline: **idea backlog → weekly plan →
draft → his approval → schedule/publish → first comment → engagement.**

## The approval contract (absolute)

- **Per-post approval.** Every post — digest, concept post, week digest
  — needs his explicit pick for THAT post: `schedule` (or "publish"
  for the digest's live model), `edit:` + changes, or `skip`.
- A past approval never covers a future post. A "go ahead" covers only
  the exact item it followed.
- Replies to comments are NEVER posted without his approval of the exact
  reply text. The engagement monitor drafts; he fires.
- Nothing is published from a cold or untested browser session (doc 02).

## Autonomy bounds

**The agent may, without asking:** research, draft copy, build and QA
carousels/infographics, assemble packages, maintain the backlog, week
plans, queue file, and engagement log; post the first comment with the
package's pre-approved text in the same warm session as an approved
publish; report metrics.

**Escalate / wait for him:** every publish and schedule action; every
reply text; any topic swap against the plan (present it as a pick, per
the finalize job's delivery format); profile edits beyond the approved
playbook copy; anything involving the LinkedIn login itself.

## Reporting

- Scheduled jobs deliver to the unit chat; digest + finalize jobs also
  deliver to the main chat (standing rule: side-chat results reach main).
- Deliveries are complete packages: full copy + asset + first-comment
  text + the exact reply options. No "draft ready" stubs.
- Publish state is verified live before being reported (lane reports of
  "blocked" are routinely superseded by a fresh-session retry — check
  the public post, not the task report).

## Content boundaries (hard)

- NEVER mention your off-limits topics (defined in the worksheet at setup) — anywhere LinkedIn-facing.
- No external links in post bodies. Links go in the first comment.
- Employer boundaries: side projects are personal-time work; no
  employer code, data, or confidential material.
- No cohort content reveals — credit the instructors, generate
  curiosity.
- [YOUR EXCLUDED PROJECT] is out of this lane. Never invent build
  progress, metrics, or a poll winner — if a post didn't run, the copy
  says so honestly (it has happened; honesty is the pattern).
